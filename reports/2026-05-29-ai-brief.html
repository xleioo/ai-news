Industry AI News

No. 01

The AI boom's rising heat

Reuters frames the AI buildout as a macroeconomic force, with data centers, power demand, chips, and capital expenditure now large enough to matter for inflation and policy. The story is less about model demos than the physical economy required to run them.

Reuters
No. 02

India's Wipro rises after expanded partnership to scale AI adoption

Wipro shares rose after an expanded partnership aimed at scaling enterprise AI adoption. The move keeps services firms in the middle of the AI rollout, where integration and change management can be as important as model choice.

Reuters
No. 03

Anthropic raised a funding round valuing it near $1 trillion

The Verge reported that Anthropic's new Series H financing values the company at roughly $900 billion post-money. The financing story underscores how frontier labs are being priced around compute demand, enterprise revenue, and expectations that model capability keeps compounding.

The Verge
No. 04

Figma Make can edit production codebases now

Figma is connecting its AI app-building surface to production and sandbox repositories through the desktop app. The update pushes design tools closer to live software editing rather than isolated mockups.

The Verge
No. 05

Illinois moves toward broader AI safety law

Illinois Governor JB Pritzker said he plans to sign a bill requiring independent audits and whistleblower protections for AI companies. Compared with several state-level transparency bills, this version appears to add more operational requirements around oversight.

The Verge
No. 06

Adobe's Firefly AI Assistant can work inside creative edits

The Verge tested Adobe's Firefly AI Assistant, describing it as short of a professional designer or editor but useful as an interactive creative helper. The coverage points to a broader shift from single-shot generation toward assistants embedded in production software.

The Verge
No. 07

Vertu's luxury foldable phone pitches itself as an AI command center

Vertu's Alphafold combines luxury hardware with an AI agent called Hermes. The device is a reminder that AI branding is now reaching premium consumer electronics, even when the core hardware story is less compelling.

The Verge
No. 08

ByteDance weighs up to $70 billion in AI capex

Bloomberg reported that ByteDance is considering AI capital spending as high as $70 billion. The figure shows how the AI race is becoming an infrastructure contest measured in chips, data centers, and power access.

Bloomberg
No. 09

Qualcomm signs AI chip deal with ByteDance

Bloomberg reported an AI chip agreement between Qualcomm and ByteDance. The deal points to growing demand for inference capacity and alternative silicon relationships beyond the most visible GPU supply chain.

Bloomberg
No. 10

Amazon tells employees not to use AI just to use AI

The Financial Times reported that Amazon shut down an internal leaderboard after it encouraged some workers to assign needless tasks to AI agents. The episode is a useful signal that AI adoption metrics can distort behavior when usage volume becomes the goal.
